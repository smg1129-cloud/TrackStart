Records-inspection FAQ

The questions boards, managers, and owners ask most about Florida's records-inspection statutes.

For associations

How quickly must we respond to a records request?

A condominium association must make records available within 10 working days of receiving a written request (§ 718.111(12)(c)); a homeowners association within 10 business days (§ 720.303(5)(a)). Missing the deadline creates a rebuttable presumption that the association willfully failed to comply.

What happens if we miss the deadline or refuse?

Both statutes provide minimum damages of $50 per day for up to 10 days (a $500 minimum), and the prevailing owner or member is entitled to recover attorney's fees. Because the presumption is of willful noncompliance, the practical exposure — especially fees — can far exceed the statutory damages.

Can we ask why the owner wants the records?

No. The association may adopt reasonable rules about the frequency, time, location, notice, and manner of inspection, but it may not require an owner to state the purpose of the request as a condition of access.

Which records must we withhold?

Attorney-client privileged material and work product, information obtained in approving a sale/lease/transfer, employee personnel records (except compensation), owners' medical records, Social Security/driver-license/credit-card numbers, protected contact information, and the association's electronic security measures, passwords, and software. See the protected-records list for full citations.

Do condominiums really have to produce a checklist now?

Yes. Since 2024, a condominium association responding to a records request must provide the requestor a checklist of the records made available and those withheld, and retain that checklist for 7 years. RecordsGuard FL generates it automatically — and we produce one for HOAs too as a best practice.

How long must we keep official records?

Most records must be kept for at least 7 years; core governing documents are kept permanently; bids for 1 year; ballots and election materials for 1 year; and structural integrity reserve studies and milestone/structural inspection reports for 15 years. For owners

How do I request to inspect records?

Submit a written request to the association. You don't have to explain why. You may inspect the records and make or obtain copies at your reasonable expense. You can start a request in our owner portal.

Can the association charge me?

The association may charge the reasonable costs of copying and, in some cases, personnel time to supervise or produce records, within statutory limits. Inspection itself is a right; copying costs are the owner's reasonable expense.

Why was part of a record redacted or withheld?

Certain information is statutorily protected — like Social Security numbers, medical records, or attorney-client material — and the association is required to withhold or redact it. Your records checklist will identify what was withheld and the statutory basis for it.
